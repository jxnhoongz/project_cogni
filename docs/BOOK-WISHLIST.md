# Cognibot book wishlist

Candidate books for the channel, tuned to what our format is now good at:
**puzzle-first** (a book with concrete "why is X?" hooks) + an **honest verdict** (a book
worth judging, ideally with real "where it's wrong" material — that section is our edge).

**How to use:** pick one, get the text, drop it in `sources/`, tell me to run the pipeline.
Check it off when shipped.

**Legend** — Source: `[PD]` public-domain (I fetch it free/legal via Gutenberg) · `[©]` in
copyright (you source it). Puzzle = how naturally it fits puzzle-first. Verdict = how juicy
the "honest verdict / where it's wrong" angle is (★ = our differentiator lands hard).

## Tier 1 — puzzle-driven pop-econ/science (closest to The Economic Naturalist)

Best fit for puzzle-first. Every one is a chain of concrete "why is X?" hooks.

- [ ] **Freakonomics** — Levitt & Dubner (2005) · `[©]` · Puzzle ★★★ · Verdict ★★★
      The canonical one. "Why do drug dealers live with their moms?" Built for puzzle-first.
      Verdict gold: the abortion-crime chapter was found to have a coding error (Foote &
      Goetz) — a real, checkable where-wrong.
- [ ] **The Undercover Economist** — Tim Harford (2005) · `[©]` · Puzzle ★★★ · Verdict ★★
      Almost a twin of Economic Naturalist — why coffee costs what it does, etc.
- [ ] **Predictably Irrational** — Dan Ariely (2008) · `[©]` · Puzzle ★★★ · Verdict ★★★
      Behavioural-econ puzzles from real experiments. Where-wrong writes itself: Ariely was
      later at the centre of a data-fabrication scandal (2021 insurance-honesty study). On
      brand for "honest verdict."
- [ ] **The Economic Naturalist** — Robert Frank (2007) · `[©]` · Puzzle ★★★ · Verdict ★★
      The 成长书库 reference book. Easy-mode puzzle-first. *(User: "want it, but not now.")*
- [ ] **Nudge** — Thaler & Sunstein (2008) · `[©]` · Puzzle ★★ · Verdict ★★

## Tier 2 — popular nonfiction where the VERDICT is the draw

These are famous, engaging, AND have a real "where it's wrong" section — which is exactly
where Cognibot beats a plain summary channel. Puzzle-first works via tension, not trivia.

- [ ] **Outliers** — Malcolm Gladwell (2008) · `[©]` · Puzzle ★★ · Verdict ★★★
      The 10,000-hour rule — genuinely oversimplified per Ericsson, the very researcher
      Gladwell cited. A clean, famous where-wrong.
- [ ] **Why We Sleep** — Matthew Walker (2017) · `[©]` · Puzzle ★★ · Verdict ★★★
      Hugely popular and hugely disputed on accuracy (Guzey's line-by-line critique). The
      verdict is the whole video.
- [ ] **Mindset** — Carol Dweck (2006) · `[©]` · Puzzle ★★ · Verdict ★★★
      Growth mindset is a replication-crisis flashpoint. Contested — verify carefully.
- [ ] **Grit** — Angela Duckworth (2016) · `[©]` · Puzzle ★★ · Verdict ★★
      Grit's predictive power is contested (meta-analyses). Real where-wrong.
- [ ] **Influence** — Robert Cialdini (1984) · `[©]` · Puzzle ★★★ · Verdict ★★
      Six weapons of persuasion — each a "why did you say yes?" puzzle. Mostly holds up.

## Tier 3 — public-domain classics (free + legal + famous = lowest confabulation risk)

I can fetch these in one command (Gutenberg). Famous → dense in training data → the where-
wrong citations are safest here. Good for stress-testing puzzle-first on the legal seam.

- [ ] **The Prince** — Machiavelli (1532) · `[PD]` · Puzzle ★★ · Verdict ★★★
      Short, brutal, endlessly argued-over. The verdict ("is he a monster or a realist?")
      is a whole video.
- [ ] **Meditations** — Marcus Aurelius (~180) · `[PD]` · Puzzle ★★ · Verdict ★★
      Riding the huge modern stoicism wave. Puzzle via tension (a Roman emperor's private
      anxieties = your Sunday-night dread).
- [ ] **The Interpretation of Dreams** — Freud (1899) · `[PD]` · Puzzle ★★ · Verdict ★★★
      Where-wrong heaven — Freud is largely discredited scientifically, and everyone knows
      the name. Maximum verdict juice, maximum documentation.
- [ ] **The Art of War** — Sun Tzu · `[PD]` · Puzzle ★★ · Verdict ★★
- [ ] **Walden** — Thoreau (1854) · `[PD]` · Puzzle ★ · Verdict ★★★
      The "simple living" saint who had his mom do his laundry — the verdict is delicious.
- [ ] **The Wealth of Nations** — Adam Smith (1776) · `[PD]` · Puzzle ★ · Verdict ★★
      Dense, but the 不略 国富国穷 teardown proves the format survives hard economic history.

## Notes on choosing

- **Verify every where-wrong claim before shipping** (web-check, per the Frankl citations).
  The critique angles above are leads to investigate, not settled facts.
- **A famous book is a safer book** — the confabulation risk drops the more the model has
  read about it. Tier 3 and the big Tier 1/2 names are safest.
- **First puzzle-first video:** a Tier 1 title shows the technique at full strength (real
  puzzles); a Tier 3 classic shows it on the legal, lowest-risk seam. Either is a good pilot.
